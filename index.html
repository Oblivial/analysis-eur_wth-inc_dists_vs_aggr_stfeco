Economic Satisfaction, Income and Wealth

This report compares country-year summaries of satisfaction with the state of the economy (ESS stfeco) with distributional series from the World Inequality Database (WID). Country-year comparisons require observations of satisfaction, income and wealth, and the validity rules below apply throughout the analysis.

Data Validity

An observation is valid for comparison only when ESS flags the country-year as reliable, both WID distributions contain all ten equal-split adult decile shares and sum to one within 2.5 percentage points, and neither WID series marks the year as extrapolated. The WID metadata does not identify all interpolated years. Observations failing any rule are excluded from every section after comparability.

Satisfaction and Income/Wealth Association

The merged table uses the earliest matched observation in each two-year ESS round and the corresponding WID distribution. It retains ESS reliability, effective sample size, the selected post-tax income series where available, and WID extrapolation flags. The full country-round table is available as data/processed/ess_wid_country_round.csv.

All summaries and correlations below use ESS observations flagged reliable. Correlations are descriptive only; no significance tests are performed. Gini values are approximated from WID’s ten decile shares. Palma is the top 10% share divided by the bottom 40% share; wealth Palma is missing when bottom-40 net wealth is zero or negative.

Income and Wealth Distribution Change

This section summarizes annualized income and wealth distribution changes for countries with matched economic satisfaction data. Years use two-year survey rounds anchored at 2002; the earliest matched observation in each round is used. Each reported transition compares consecutive selected survey observations, skipping intervening distribution years. WID post-tax national-income shares are used where available, with pre-tax shares as a fallback. For each transition, the score is the mean absolute change across the nine cumulative decile-share cutoffs, divided by the gap between survey observations. It is a magnitude in percentage points per year; it does not indicate whether inequality rose or fell. The metric uses the cumulative share profile directly, so it also applies when net wealth shares are negative.

Satisfaction Change Against Distribution Change

These transition-level comparisons relate the annualized absolute satisfaction mean change to the annualized income or wealth distribution-change magnitude. Same-interval comparisons match identical endpoint years. Lagged comparisons match both distribution endpoints 3 survey rounds earlier than the satisfaction endpoints; missing rounds are not skipped. Only reliable satisfaction transitions and non-extrapolated distribution transitions are included. Both measures are magnitudes, so these associations do not show whether satisfaction or inequality rose or fell. Correlations are descriptive.

Economic Satisfaction Change

Satisfaction changes are calculated from the survey summaries. The mean measures movement in average satisfaction with the state of the economy; the IQR measures change in spread. Both are annualized absolute changes in satisfaction scale points. Years use two-year survey rounds anchored at 2002, with the earliest available valid matched observation in each round. Transitions compare consecutive selected observations; gaps may span more than one round.

Largest Satisfaction Mean and IQR Changes

The largest mean changes are ranked by annualized magnitude. The IQR panel shows the observed IQR at both survey endpoints for the largest annualized IQR changes, making the before/after spread visible. Segment color indicates whether the IQR widened or narrowed; an asterisk marks an unreliable or unclassified endpoint.
